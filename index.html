What this stack is for

The job to be done

This is not a general office AI rollout. It is a small production studio. The team takes dense clinical and federal guidance on opioid use disorder and turns it into training people will finish and remember.

What the team makes

●Micro-learning videos. Short, captioned, plain language training modules.
●Infographics. One look explainers of protocols, screening, and workflows.
●Resource kits. Job aids, quick reference sheets, and facilitator guides.

Who it serves

●OUD treatment agencies that need staff trained quickly and consistently.
●Paraprofessionals and peers who learn best from short, visual, digestible content.
●Technical assistance providers who reuse these assets in the field.

Because the deliverables are visual and video first, the creative and authoring tools are the core of this stack, not an afterthought. That shapes every recommendation that follows.

Read this first

Two facts about UNR that shape the plan

1. UNR is a Microsoft campus, not Google

UNR moved email and core productivity from Google to Microsoft 365 in 2024, citing security. OIT officially recommends Microsoft Copilot. That makes Copilot Chat your no cost, no friction starting point for drafting and Q and A, because it already lives inside your protected tenant.

2. There is an AI policy with real guardrails

UNR holds no formal AI vendor contracts. Any new paid AI tool must pass an OIT vendor risk assessment first, and no regulated data may enter consumer AI tools. Training content is low risk, but OUD work carries HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 duties, so real patient data stays out.

Why this helps the budget: the everyday tool UNR already blesses is free, and every paid tool here is used only on training content, never on protected records. That keeps cost and compliance risk low. Reference: UNR OIT AI Usage Policy.

How the stack was chosen

Six rules for a novice production team

✓Compliance first. Start with what OIT approves, keep HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 data out of consumer tools.
✓Template driven. Favor tools where a beginner starts from a template, not a blank page.
✓Free before paid. Prove value on no cost tiers, then buy seats only where production demands it.
✓Pool, do not spray. Share creative seats across the team instead of licensing everyone.
✓Accessibility built in. Captions and plain language by default, because the audience needs it.
✓Annual billing. Where a tool earns its place, annual plans cut 15 to 38 percent off monthly.

The goal is the stack a beginner team will still be using in six months, producing real assets, at a cost the school can renew without a fight.

A 5 minute demo script

1Upload two or three SAMHSA OUD guidance PDFs into a new notebook.
2Ask "Summarize this for a new peer support specialist in plain language."
3Show citations. Every answer points back to the exact source passage.
4Generate an Audio Overview. A podcast style recap the team can play in the car.
5Land the point: this is the distill step of the whole workflow, and it costs the school nothing.

You can start near zero this quarter and only commit real dollars once the team has shipped assets. That is an easy story to tell a budget committee.

Compliance checklist

Keep it clean with OIT

✓Run the OIT vendor risk assessment for each paid tool before purchase. Build two weeks of lead time in.
✓Training content only. No real patient records, case notes, or PHI in any consumer AI tool. HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 apply to OUD data.
✓Prefer tenant protected tools for anything sensitive. Copilot inside M365 keeps data in your tenant.
✓Clinician review every asset. AI drafts, a licensed reviewer approves, before anything reaches the field.
✓Buy under a department account, not personal cards, so seats and renewals stay centrally managed.
✓Label AI generated media and keep a one page tool register for next year's budget and audit.

None of this is heavy. It is the difference between a studio that survives its first compliance review and one that gets switched off. Reference: UNR AI Usage Policy.
